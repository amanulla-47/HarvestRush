# HARVEST RUSH — Story

The hero's name and pronouns are chosen by the player; the text below uses "he" for the default,
and the in-game text uses placeholders so every chapter reads correctly for he, she or they.
In the game each chapter appears first as a one- or two-line beat; the full text is optional.

A farm that was almost lost, and the boy who refused to let it go.

## The characters

- **The hero** (named by the player; Ravi by default) — Nineteen, thin, stubborn, kind. Wears his father's conical straw hat;
  it is a size too big and he has never let anyone fix it. The wheat sprig in the band was his
  mother's habit: "so the field always knows you are coming home."
- **Baba** — Ravi's father. Never seen alive. He built the scarecrow, the stall counter, and the
  dream of a farm that could feed the whole village. He died in the dry year, owing money.
- **Meera** — the market keeper. Loud, soft-hearted, the first person to believe in Ravi.
- **Dev** — the hero's childhood friend who lost his own land. The first farmhand.
- **Priya** (named by the player) — the new schoolteacher. Practical, funny, and the first person
  who argues with the hero instead of pitying him. She buys the thinnest wheat on purpose, feeds
  the chickens without being asked, and marries him under the festival lanterns.
- **The village** — children, neighbours, and old friends who return one by one as the farm grows.

## Prologue — The Hat (game start)

The rain stopped the week Baba died, and it did not come back for a year.

Ravi stands at the gate of what is left: one field of wheat gone wild, a fence with more
gaps than posts, a market stall with its awning in rags, and a scarecrow his father built
with a shirt that still smells faintly of him.

There is a letter in his pocket from the moneylender. Thirty days.

He puts on the hat. It is too big. It has always been too big.

"Okay, Baba," he says to nobody. "One field. We start with one field."

## Chapter 1 — One Field of Wheat (first harvest and first sale)

The wheat is thin but it is his. He cuts it by hand, stacks it in the old wicker basket on
his back, and walks it to the market in the dark so nobody will see how little there is.

Meera sees. Meera sees everything.

She counts out his coins slowly, like they matter. "Your father used to bring me wheat like
this," she says. "The first year. Before it was good."

Ravi does not answer, because if he answers he will cry, and he has decided that he is done
crying. He walks home with two coins and sows the field again before he sleeps.

## Chapter 2 — Seeds on Credit (Carrot field unlocked)

The carrot field behind the house is nothing but hard dirt and a rusted sprinkler head.

Ravi has no seeds and no money for seeds. He stands at Meera's counter and cannot make the
words come out.

She pushes a paper packet across the wood. "Carrots. Pay me when they come up."

"And if they don't come up?"

"Then you will owe me carrots," she says, "and you will plant them again."

That night he breaks the dirt with his hands because the hoe is broken too. The chickens,
who have nobody else, follow him up and down the rows.

## Chapter 3 — A Friend Comes Back (first farmhand hired)

Dev shows up at the gate with a cloth bag and no land.

"The bank took it," he says, and shrugs like it is weather. "I heard you were planting."

The hero can pay him almost nothing. Dev takes almost nothing. They work the fields until
the moon is up, and for the first time since the funeral there are two voices in the house
at dinner.

At the market, a woman in a lilac kurta buys the thinnest wheat on Meera's counter, on
purpose. "Someone has to," she says, and argues with him about the price. Upwards.

Meera tells him afterwards that she is the new schoolteacher, and tells him her name.
(The player names her here. Priya by default.)

## Chapter 4 — Rain (moneylender's debt paid)

He pays the moneylender with coins still warm from Meera's hand. He does not tell anyone.

That week the old sprinkler coughs, spits rust, and then, for the first time in a year,
water arcs over the field and catches the sun. Priya brings the schoolchildren to stand in
it, and lets them get soaked, and gets soaked herself.

She notices that he is still barefoot. She says nothing. The next week there are sandals on
the step.

Meera writes his name in chalk on her counter, and the stall gets its first new awning in
eleven years.

## Chapter 5 — The Fields Breathe (Pumpkin field unlocked)

There are more people on the farm now, and more chickens, and a cat nobody admits to feeding.

Priya starts coming by after school to feed the chickens. Nobody asked her to. It is the
first time anyone has looked after something on this farm without being asked, and he finds
that he walks the long way round so he can pass her twice.

The pumpkins go in where Baba always said pumpkins should go, and he realises he is walking
the rows the way his father walked them, hands behind his back, counting under his breath.

"There is a patch of ground by the market," Dev says, to nobody in particular, "that would
fit a small house."

## Chapter 6 — Baba's Scarecrow (Strawberry field unlocked)

The storm takes the scarecrow down in the night. He finds it face down in the mud, the old
shirt torn through, and sits beside it for a long time while the others pretend not to watch.

Then Meera arrives with needle and thread, Dev with a new post, and Priya with the children
and a crow they have made from a sock. They put him back up together, taller than before.

He plants strawberries at his feet. "So the birds have something better to steal," he says,
and laughs, and it is the first real laugh anyone has heard from him. Priya laughs too, and
does not stop for a while.

## Chapter 7 — The Wedding (Corn field unlocked and the home built)

Bunting goes up between the market poles. Lanterns at the corners. The whole village comes,
because the two of them are getting married under them, with the little house behind the
stall still smelling of fresh paint.

Meera cries loudly and denies it. Dev plays the drum badly. The corn is taller than the
children, and the children do not come out of it until dark.

Meera finds him at the edge of the light. "Your father talked about this. Exactly this. He
never saw it."

"He's seeing it," he says, and touches the brim of the hat.

## Chapter 8 — Everyone's Farm (Grapes and Melon fields unlocked, the farm complete)

The grapes come purple. The melons come heavy. The moneylender's letter is still in the
drawer, and he keeps it there on purpose.

On the morning the last field is sown, he takes the wheat sprig from his hatband, and Priya
takes his hand, and they plant it at the gate together, the way his mother did every spring.
"So the field knows."

Then they turn around. Dev is arguing with a chicken. Meera is shouting prices at nobody.
The children are chasing butterflies through the corn. The scarecrow stands over all of it
with its stitched shirt and its sock crow.

It is not his farm. It never was. It is everyone's, and that is exactly what Baba wanted.

He pulls the hat down. Priya picks up the basket. They go to work.

## How the story is meant to meet the game

| Game moment | Story beat | What the player sees |
|---|---|---|
| New game | Prologue: The Hat | Run-down farm: broken fence, ragged awning, one wild wheat field, debt letter |
| First sale | Chapter 1 | Meera's first words; the stall gets its chalk sign |
| Carrot field | Chapter 2 | Seeds on credit; chickens arrive |
| Carrot field + 30 sales | Chapter 3 | Dev walks in free as the first farmhand; Priya buys the thin wheat; the player names her |
| Moneylender debt paid (300 coins) | Chapter 4 | New awning; hero's name chalked on the counter; patched clothes replaced |
| Pumpkin field | Chapter 5 | Priya feeds the chickens after school; the home mat appears |
| Strawberry field | Chapter 6 | Scarecrow rebuilt by everyone, taller |
| Corn field + home built | Chapter 7 | The wedding under the bunting and lanterns |
| Grapes and Melon | Chapter 8 | They plant the wheat sprig at the gate together; full farm |

Each chapter is written to fit on one or two story cards in the game, with Ravi's hat and
Baba's scarecrow as the two objects that carry the emotion from the first screen to the last.

## After the story: the Harvest Festival

When every field is open and the last chapter is told, Meera says the village would come for a
Harvest Festival. Holding it ends the season: fireworks over the square, then a quiet winter.
The hero and the partner hang a festival ribbon on the farm gate. The fields rest, the crew goes
home, and in spring the work starts again from one field of wheat. This time the market pays more
for a farm with a ribbon. Each festival adds another rosette to the gate, a small record of
every year the farm fed the village.
